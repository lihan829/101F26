## A. Course Description

3 hr.; 3 cr. A survey of the study of language: structure, language and society, first and second language acquisition, and other related topics.

## B. Course Goals / Objectives

The goal of this course is to introduce students to linguistics — the scientific study of human language. By the end of this course, students should:

1. Be able to analyze basic linguistic structures (sounds, words, and sentences);
2. Be able to engage in linguistic argumentation using data from English and other languages;
3. Be familiar with current research on how the human brain learns, processes, and produces language;
4. Understand how language use constitutes social meaning, and its relationship to identity and variation in contemporary society.

**Queens College General Education Statement:** This course satisfies the following five Queens College Pathways criteria:

- **QC 1:** Address how, in the discipline of the course, data and evidence are construed and knowledge is acquired — how questions are asked and answered.
- **QC 2:** Position the discipline in the liberal arts curriculum and the larger society.
- **QC 3:** Understand and use the concepts and methods of the discipline.
- **QC 4:** Gather, interpret, and assess information from various sources, and evaluate arguments critically.
- **QC 5:** Solve problems, support conclusions, or defend insights.

As a QC College Option **LANG** course, this course also satisfies:

- **LANG 1:** Differentiate types of language and appreciate their structures.
- **LANG 2:** Relate language, thought, and culture.
- **LANG 3:** Compare natural languages, formal languages, and logic.
- **LANG 4:** Understand the processes involved in learning languages.

## D. Assignments, Due Dates, and Grading Plan

There are three components to the assessment for this course:

1. **Class participation — 5%.** Based on attendance and participation in class discussion.
2. **Exams (3) — 25% each (75% total).** Two of these are non-cumulative unit exams; the third takes place during finals week and is partially cumulative. If you need to miss an exam, you must request permission **in advance**. If you fail to submit an exam by its due date without prior arrangement, you receive a zero for that exam.
3. **Homework assignments (10) — 20% total.** Administered via Blackboard. Homework is graded pass/fail: to receive a "pass" you must answer at least 25% of the questions correctly by the due date. Your grade is based on your six **highest** results — in other words, you must pass at least six of the ten to receive full credit for this component. You are welcome to collaborate with classmates on homework.

Late assignments will only be accepted with prior approval from the instructor.
